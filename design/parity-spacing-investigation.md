# Parity-specific fertility: spacing calibration investigation

October 29, 2026. This records the diagnosis and alternatives that led to the
implemented D101 cell-accounting mechanism, followed by D102 fertility weights.
Milestone 5 is accepted complete under D105. The original D099 correction below
is superseded; [cell accounting](fertility-cell-accounting.md) and
[heterogeneity](fertility-heterogeneity-notes.md) describe current behavior.
The [accepted report](../validation/milestone5.qmd) remains the user's scientific
assessment. Historical diagnostic evidence does not imply every proposed
alternative was adopted or every target is feasible.

## Evidence from the user's validation report

The user measured the last 50 years of a 100-year simulation, over 100
replications. At age [18,19), supplied rates for current parities 0,1,2,3,4+
were .0592, .133, .135, .110, .149. With positive birth spacing, estimated means
were .0588, .0856, .0611, .0235, 0; corresponding MCSEs were .000218, .000713,
.00186, .00665, 0. The last category had exposure in only 15 replications and
is too sparse for a reliable recovery claim.

After setting `birth_interval = 0`, the user reported means .0594, .134, .133,
.120, .151, with MCSEs .000173, .000828, .00237, .00656, .0347. All 100
replications contributed except parity 4+, with 99. These values were supplied
in conversation; they were not regenerated by the agent and should not replace
the user-authored report. The original positive-spacing interval value was not
stated alongside that table. The report remains untouched by this investigation.

Together with the [independent initialized-cohort diagnostic](../validation/milestone5/README.md),
these findings support birth-spacing compensation as the source of the large
systematic shortfall, rather than an error in parity switching or maternal
exposure extraction. Burn-in alone has not resolved the issue. Sparse cells
still require their exposure and number of contributing replications to be
reported; a zero estimated MCSE in sparse zero-event cells is not evidence of
precise recovery.

## Interpretation

Conditional fertility inputs use all woman-years in an age/parity cell, including
spacing-ineligible time. Simulation generates births only among eligible women.
The correction therefore needs the eligible fraction within the *current* cell.
A preceding-parity rate by itself does not generally identify that fraction;
inflows, the stock at the current parity, and recent birth histories also matter.
Young women at higher parities necessarily have experienced several births in
relatively little time, so the discrepancy can be particularly large there.

Initialization can affect the adjustment through unusual parity flows, but it
does not invalidate an exposure-based rate estimate. Recovery without spacing
supports the software accounting; it does not validate the positive-spacing
correction. The current SOCSIM-style approximation is not an exact general
eligibility correction.

## Composition diagnosis of the preceding-parity correction

Analysis added October 29, 2026 at the user's request. This diagnoses the
shortfall and records a candidate correction; it is not an accepted model.

**Identity.** For actual parity k >= 1, a woman enters the cell only by a birth
and cannot leave it during the following spacing interval B, apart from death.
The ineligible woman-years in cell (a, k) are therefore exactly the entrants
from parity k-1 during the last B years. With N_k(a) women at parity k:

    ineligible_k(a) = [births to parity k-1 women during (a-B, a]] / N_k(a)
                    ~= B * m_{k-1}(a) * N_{k-1}(a) / N_k(a)

D099 and SOCSIM use B * m_{k-1}(a), which implicitly sets N_{k-1}/N_k = 1.
Parity zero has no ineligible time, so it is correctly unadjusted.

**Why this explains the observed pattern.**

- Unstratified fertility (D028): numerator and denominator are the same women,
  so the ratio is one and the midpoint correction is appropriate. This is
  consistent with the good milestone 1 recovery.
- Parity: every birth moves the mother into the next stratum, the worst case for
  the implicit ratio. At young ages N_{k-1}/N_k is large for higher k, so most
  parity-k women at, say, age 18 have just entered and are ineligible. This
  matches the near-zero 4+ rates at age [18,19) in Hammel and SOCSIM. Over
  broad settled ages the ratio is closer to one, matching the near-input
  parity-one recovery at ages [20,35) in SOCSIM's later windows.
- Milestone 2 partnered fertility and the milestone 1 restart transient are
  other failures of the same identity: recent births occurred in a different
  stratum or under a different schedule from the one being corrected.

**Candidate composition-based correction.** Run the target conditional rates
through a deterministic synthetic cohort (the machinery behind
`calculate_patfr()`) to obtain parity proportions l_k(a), then

    e_k(a) = 1 - integral_{a-B}^{a} m_{k-1}(s) l_{k-1}(s) ds / l_k(a)
    h_k(a) = m_k(a) / e_k(a)

For a pooled open top parity, the numerator also includes repeat births within
that category; tracking actual parity (as D099 already does) avoids this
complication. The construction uses only inputs, with no simulation feedback:
if observed parity k-1 rates equal their targets, parity k entrants and stock
follow the target cohort, so the correction holds inductively from parity zero.
With parity-independent mortality and constant rates, survival cancels from the
ratio and a long-run population's age-parity composition equals the synthetic
cohort's.

**Standalone evidence.** [check-composition-spacing.R](../validation/milestone5/check-composition-spacing.R)
is an independent discrete-time toy simulation, not engine code: an initialized
cohort of 40,000 women at parity zero, targets .2/.3/.4 (2+), B = .75, ten years,
no mortality, one replication (seed 9601). Results at dt = .0025
([summary CSV](../validation/milestone5/composition-spacing-summary.csv)):

| Correction | Parity 0 | Parity 1 | Parity 2+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None, B = 0 (control) | .2004 | .3028 | .3989 |
| Preceding parity (D099/SOCSIM) | .1988 | .2647 | .3357 |
| Composition-based | .1990 | .3016 | .3959 |

The preceding-parity row reproduces Hammel's initialized-cohort shortfall
(.266/.333). The composition-based correction recovers the targets about as
closely as the zero-spacing control. A rerun at dt = .001 gave .2014/.3007/.3985,
so the residuals are consistent with Monte Carlo variation (parity-one SE about
.002) rather than step-size bias. Here the initialized cohort *is* the synthetic
cohort, so this is the case in which the correction should be exact; it does
not test later windows or other compositions.

**Limitations and open decisions.**

1. *Composition mismatch.* Exactness requires the simulated age-parity
   composition to match the synthetic cohort. That fails during burn-in from a
   baseline without birth histories (all women start at parity zero), after a
   restart with a new schedule, and when group or union-type membership changes.
   Those cases would need runtime eligible-exposure accounting, the
   population-ratio approach that requires user agreement.
2. *Infeasible targets.* e_k(a) can approach or fall below zero when target
   rates imply more recent entrants than the cell's stock, which is impossible
   under a B-year minimum interval. The toy cohort's earliest parity cells reach
   e = 0 (they have no eligible women, so no hazard is applied). Whether such
   schedules are rejected, warned about or capped is a modeling decision.
3. *Scope.* Parity-only deterministic preparation versus the broader
   eligibility-calibration framework below, in which union types and restarts
   need runtime accounting.
4. *Untested.* Later windows of a population with mortality and age structure,
   age-varying empirical schedules, upper-open age bands, and group multipliers.

## General candidate: runtime cell accounting

Analysis added October 29, 2026 at the user's request, after the composition
diagnosis above. This records a candidate mechanism and toy evidence; it is not
an accepted model or authorization to change the engine.

**Why a general fix must use the population.** An input rate is births per
woman-year over *all* women in a cell (age band x parity band x group x union
type), including those inside their birth interval. That makes it a population
quantity. Any correction based only on a woman's own history or a precomputed
table (D028, D099, the composition-based correction) assumes some cell mix.
Recovery under an arbitrary mix (non-stable populations, changing or restarted
schedules, random baselines, stratified rates) requires the mix the simulation
actually has.

**Mechanism.** Give each cell c a total birth intensity m_c * N_c, where N_c
counts all women currently in the cell, and assign each birth to a randomly
chosen eligible woman in it. Equivalently, each eligible woman has hazard
m_c * N_c / E_c, with E_c eligible women. Expected births per woman-year equal
m_c by construction whenever E_c > 0, with no assumption about composition.
Unstratified fertility is the special case in which cells are age bands, so
this could replace both D028 and D099.

**Toy evidence.** [check-runtime-spacing.R](../validation/milestone5/check-runtime-spacing.R)
is an independent discrete-time toy, not engine code: an initialized cohort of
40,000 women at parity zero, B = .75, no mortality, one replication (seed 7).
Targets are .2/.3/.4 (2+) before t = 5 and 1.6 times those afterwards. The
composition-based correction keeps its table from the original schedule, so its
assumed mix is wrong after the change. Rates for parities 0 / 1 / 2+
([summary CSV](../validation/milestone5/runtime-spacing-summary.csv)):

| Window | Targets | No spacing (control) | Composition-based | Runtime accounting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [0,5) | .20 / .30 / .40 | .202 / .296 / .401 | .200 / .300 / .386 | .201 / .284 / .390 |
| [5,5.5) | .32 / .48 / .64 | .321 / .481 / .637 | .330 / .448 / .563 | .325 / .495 / .652 |
| [5.5,6) | .32 / .48 / .64 | .317 / .471 / .651 | .311 / .429 / .498 | .324 / .475 / .629 |
| [6,8) | .32 / .48 / .64 | .326 / .477 / .640 | .327 / .434 / .530 | .325 / .474 / .640 |

Runtime accounting follows the schedule change within half a year; the
composition-based correction does not. Single-replication differences in the
third decimal are within Monte Carlo variation.

Both methods' shortfalls in [0,5) come from targets that are infeasible early
in an initialized cohort, not from the methods themselves:

- During the first .75 years every parity-one woman has just given birth, and
  before t = 1.5 no woman can be at parity 2+ *and* eligible (two births at
  least .75 apart, then a further .75 years). A finer-step rerun of the
  composition method (dt = .0005) gave a parity-2+ rate of 0 in [0,1.5) and
  .4017 in [1.5,5); parity one was .2927 and .3018.
- The runtime script records the intensity assigned to cells with no eligible
  women as expected lost births: 645 at parity one and 224 at 2+ in [0,5).
  Adding them back gives (13,520 + 645) / 47,601 = .298 and
  (10,211 + 224) / 26,157 = .399.

**Possible implementation (not selected).** The engine already maintains a
similar structure for matching calibration ([current-hazard contributions](calibration-intensity.md)):
counts by group and birth-time bin, with a boundary queue for age-band starts
and ends and birth-spacing releases, rebuilt from histories at restart. A
fertility tracker would hold N_c and E_c for each fertility-input cell and
update them at births (mother moves to the next parity cell, ineligible),
spacing releases, age-band crossings, deaths, group and union-type transitions,
and schedule-period changes. Unlike the matching tracker, it would decide
*when* births occur: each cell keeps one exponential waiting time at
intensity m_c * N_c, redrawn when N_c or m_c changes (valid by
memorylessness), replacing per-woman fertility waiting times. The matching
tracker's eligible fertility contributions would then follow from the cell
intensity.

**Design questions raised by the toy (resolved by D101/D102).**

1. *Population dependence.* A woman's hazard would rise when others in her cell
   recently gave birth. This is a calibration device rather than a behavioral
   model, and it is noisy in small populations or finely stratified cells.
2. *Cells with no eligible women.* Drop births and report a diagnostic, defer
   them, or error. Temporary after initialization; persistent cases mean the
   schedule cannot be met under the minimum interval (shared with the
   composition-based correction).
3. *Fertility heterogeneity.* D102 selects persistent lognormal weights for
   within-cell assignment, not direct multipliers of cell rates.
4. *Scope.* Replace D028/D099 everywhere (changing milestone 1–4 behavior and
   requiring re-validation) or offer it as an option. Fixed-seed histories
   change either way.
5. *Union-linked births.* Check cancellations, father choice and on-demand
   search, where a birth can fail after assignment.

Untested in the toy: mortality and age structure, later windows of a
generational population (including the age-18 case), union-type transitions,
small populations and sparse cells.

## Proposed specification

The user asked for the runtime cell-accounting candidate to be written as a
scientific specification, designed to admit individual fertility weights for
future heterogeneity. See [cell-accounted fertility](fertility-cell-accounting.md).
The user accepted it with all six modeling decisions on October 29, 2026 (D101).
After validation the user selected 1-year, actual-parity cells and then made
cell accounting the only method, removing midpoint compensation and D099's
correction.

## Implemented outcome and remaining scope

D101 selects runtime population accounting with per-cell birth clocks, fixed
rate-times-member intensity, and dropped/reported intensity when nobody is
eligible. Cells distinguish actual parity even within open input categories.
D102 supplies weighted eligible-mother selection and D104 defines disabled
weight lifecycle. The user accepted the combined milestone under D105.

The new mechanism removes the earlier precomputed-correction assumption. It
does not make every input schedule feasible, create actual births from lost
intensity, reconstruct missing histories or enforce a stationary inherited
weight distribution. The accepted report shows realized and unrealized-adjusted
rates separately; its latter measure is an accounting diagnostic.

Individual group transitions are now implemented and accepted under D116;
see [their contract](group-transitions.md) for histories and state updates.
Calendar-varying rates are implemented under [the D118 contract](time-varying-rates.md);
scientific assessment and matching/inheritance schedules remain pending. The rejected
composition-only correction and former midpoint implementation remain in Git
history and supporting diagnostics, not current execution rules.
