# Cell-accounted fertility under birth spacing

**Status: accepted and implemented (D101, October 29, 2026); the only
birth-spacing method; milestone 5 accepted under D105.** Written at the user's
request after the [spacing investigation](parity-spacing-investigation.md). It was first an
opt-in (`spacing_method = "cell"`) alongside midpoint compensation
(D028/D099); after reviewing the validation below, the user removed midpoint
and the argument. The midpoint comparisons in this document are reproducible at
commit `89d451b`. After validation the user also revised decision 5: cells are
age sub-bands of at most one year and, for parity-specific schedules, single
actual parities. See [implementation and validation](#implementation-and-validation).
Milestone 8 (D128, accepted under D130) moved births to the fertility process's
single clock shared by all its cells, pooled groups when fertility schedules
are not group-specific, and changed unrealized births from expected values to
counts; this document describes that current behavior. Formula and custom
fertility weights (D129) combine with `hetfert` on the log scale.

## Purpose

Fertility inputs are births per woman-year over *all* women in a stratum,
including women inside their minimum birth interval. The simulation can only
assign births to eligible women. The former precomputed corrections raised eligible
women's hazards by an amount computed from the input schedule alone, which
assumes a particular mix of eligible and ineligible women. When the simulated
mix differed, the input rates were not recovered. Known cases:

- parity-specific rates, especially at young ages (milestone 5);
- union-type rates when women change type during the interval (milestone 2, D045);
- schedule changes at restart (milestone 1);
- initial populations without birth histories (milestone 1 first-year excess).

This specification replaces precomputed compensation with accounting of the
simulated population itself. It is designed to admit individual fertility
weights (future heterogeneity) without changing the mechanism.

## Definitions and units

- **Fertility cell** `c`: a set of women who face the same input rate at the
  current time. Cells come from the effective fertility schedules (the input
  table after group multipliers), identified by group and union type (or
  overall). Each input age band is split at whole ages and at its own edges,
  so a cell spans at most one year: [15,40) gives [15,16), ..., [39,40), and
  [15.5,17) gives [15.5,16) and [16,17). An upper-open band is split up to the
  oldest age attainable in the run and then left open. With parity-specific
  schedules, every actual parity has its own cells, and a pooled band such as
  [4,Inf) applies its rate to each actual parity 4, 5, ...; without parity
  columns, cells pool all parities. Ages outside any band belong to no cell and
  have zero fertility. A woman belongs to at most one cell at any time.
- **Cell rate** `m_c`: the effective input rate (births per woman-year) after
  group multiplication. No spacing adjustment is applied to it.
- **Members** `N_c`: living women currently in the cell, eligible or not.
- **Eligible members**: members whose time since their last actual birth is at
  least the birth interval `B` (years), or who have no recorded birth.
- **Individual weight** `w_i`: a finite, nonnegative relative fertility weight.
  It is 1 by default; D102 defines persistent lognormal `hetfert` weights.
  `W_c` is the summed weight of all members and `W_c^e` the summed weight of
  eligible members.
- **Cell intensity** `Lambda_c`: expected births per year generated by the cell.

## Rule

1. **Cell intensity.** `Lambda_c = m_c * N_c` (decision 4 below). Expected births per woman-year in the cell therefore
   equal `m_c` at every instant at which the cell has an eligible member with
   positive weight, whatever the mix of eligible and ineligible women.
2. **Timing.** The fertility process has one clock spent at the total
   intensity `sum_c Lambda_c`, competing with the clocks of the other rate
   processes (D128). When it fires, a uniform draw picks cell c with
   probability `Lambda_c / sum Lambda`.
3. **Choice of mother.** In the chosen cell, one eligible member is
   chosen with probability `w_i / W_c^e`. With all weights 1 this is a uniform
   draw among eligible members.
4. **Equivalent individual hazard.** Rules 1–3 are equivalent to giving each
   eligible woman the hazard `h_i = Lambda_c * w_i / W_c^e`, and zero during
   spacing. Two eligible women in the same cell therefore have relative risk
   `w_i / w_j` exactly. This form is used for interpretation, matching
   calibration and validation; the process clock is the computational form.
5. **After a successful birth.** The mother's parity rises by one if parity is
   tracked, she becomes ineligible until `t + B`, and she moves to the cell for
   her new parity (if different) as an ineligible member. Both affected cells'
   counts change at that instant.
6. **Births that fail after the mother is chosen.** On-demand partner search
   and rate-mode nonmarital father selection keep their current rules. A
   cancelled birth leaves the woman's parity, last birth and eligibility
   unchanged. The intensity that produced the attempt counts as spent: partner
   scarcity may reduce realized fertility, and this rule must not silently
   restore those births (existing scientific contract).
7. **Mortality** is unaffected. Women remain at mortality risk during spacing.

## Count updates

`N_c`, `W_c` and the eligible totals change only at these events. After each
change, the affected cell intensities are recomputed and the process clock re-timed.

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Successful birth | Mother leaves her parity cell (if parity changes) and enters the next as ineligible; newborn girls enter a cell when they reach the first fertility band |
| Spacing release at `t + B` | Mother becomes eligible in her current cell |
| Age-band boundary | Woman moves to the next band's cell, or leaves fertility ages, keeping her eligibility status |
| Death | Woman leaves her cell |
| Union formation, dissolution, widowhood, conversion | Woman moves between union-type cells, keeping her eligibility status |
| Group change | Woman moves between group cells when cells are group-specific |
| Weight change (weight-schedule switch) | Stored weights updated; no cell change |
| Restart | All cells rebuilt from supplied histories; no transient adjustment |

Boundaries reuse the existing bookkeeping-queue pattern from
[matching calibration](calibration-intensity.md): absolute calendar times,
half-open bands at exact boundaries, at most one pending boundary entry per
woman. Updates are bookkeeping, not demographic events.

**Clock updates without extra random draws.** The process clock holds an
Exp(1) threshold, drawn when total intensity first becomes positive or after a
firing, and consumes `sum Lambda * elapsed` as time passes. When any
`Lambda_c` changes, the firing time is recomputed from the unused remainder. By
memorylessness this has the same distribution as redrawing, and random numbers
are consumed only at firings (threshold, cell and mother). See
[event timing](waiting-time-sampling.md).

**Ties.** Death keeps priority over birth. A woman who dies at the exact time
the fertility clock fires is removed before the mother is chosen. Other tie rules (birth,
dissolution, conversion, search) apply to the chosen mother as they do now.

## Implementation and validation

Implemented October 29, 2026 in `src/simulate.cpp` (`BoundaryQueue`,
`FertilityCells`, `fertility_strata`) and `R/set_process.R`/`R/simulate.R`.

**Current structure (D128).** Fertility cells are one `FertilityCells`
process: an `IntensityTree` over cells, one `ProcessClock` and a
`BoundaryQueue` for age-band moves and spacing releases, which are
bookkeeping processed in time order before any event at or after them (at an
exact tie the boundary comes first, so the new half-open band applies). Each
membership or eligibility change first spends the clock for the elapsed
interval, then applies the change and re-times the clock. Union changes move a
woman between cells. Matching calibration forms fertility totals from the
cells: each cell keeps the weight of unpartnered eligible members per
calibration bin and contributes `Lambda_c / W_c^e` per unit weight. The
original October 29 implementation used one clock per cell in an indexed heap
and shared its boundary queue with the former calibration tracker; Git history
has its details.

**Mother selection** is uniform among eligible members by default. D102 uses
a per-cell sum tree over eligible swap-remove slots for selection proportional
to persistent `hetfert` weights, combined with any weight model (D129). Insertions/removals update tree ancestors, and
matching-calibration bins accumulate the same eligible weights. Cell intensity
remains rate times member count; weights do not rescale it.

**Cell construction.** Cells split by group only when the effective
fertility schedules are group-specific (D128). Zero-rate input bands create no cells and are treated as
gaps, so women there need no membership updates. Input age bands are split at whole ages and band edges;
upper-open bands are split up to the oldest attainable age in the run (end time
minus the earliest birth time) and then left open. At the user's request, an
upper-open band stays a single cell when `birth_interval * rate` is below
0.001: at most that share of its members can be in spacing, so splitting could
not change recovery materially. This is a numerical tolerance
(`kOpenBandSplitTolerance`), not a demographic parameter; HFD's 55+ rates fall
well below it. Actual-parity strata are
created when a woman first reaches a parity, so no maximum parity is assumed.
Because cells no longer follow the supplied band widths, one [15,40) band,
single-year bands and separate actual-parity bands with the same rates produce
identical histories.

**Checks.** `tests/testthat/test-fertility-cells.R` covers the API and
metadata, exact unrealized-intensity cases (one with 1-year age cells and one
with an actual-parity cell inside a pooled band), zero spacing, unstratified
recovery from a no-history start (where midpoint overshoots in the first
year), pooled upper-parity recovery, groups, union types, cohabitation,
dissolution, matching, restarts and cancellation. The internal option
`options(hammel.check_cells = TRUE)` rescans cell counts at every event and
calibration totals at every fertility-driven search; the test suite runs a
grouped rate-mode model and a grouped on-demand model with it.

Comparisons with midpoint below were produced while both methods existed (commit
`89d451b`; `validation/milestone5/check-cell-spacing.R` and
`benchmark-cell-spacing.R` require that commit).

**Recovery evidence** with 1-year, actual-parity cells (10 seeds per method;

- *Start-up.* In the initialized cohort, no woman can be eligible at parity 1
  before .75 years (mean 48 unrealized parity-1 births per run, 18 at 2+).
- *Structurally infeasible cells.* Starting at 15 with a .75-year interval, a
  woman reaches parity 5 no earlier than age 18 and cannot be eligible before
  18.75, so actual-parity cells 5+ at age 18 rarely have an eligible member.
  This lowers the observed 4+ rate at age 18 (.345).

Union and group schedules were already recovered closely by midpoint in these
scenarios; the restart blip is removed.

**Why cells are narrow (decision 5).** The first implementation used input
bands as cells. In the generational scenario with one [15,40) band, a cell
pinned the band's average, but uniform selection among eligible members moved
births toward ages where more members were eligible: age 18 parity 2+ was .283
and ages 20–35 parity 1 was .343. Supplying narrower bands showed that 1-year,
actual-parity cells recover the rates (age 18 actual parities 2/3/4+: .401,
.395, .388 with a pooled [4,Inf) cell), while 0.25-year cells increased
unrealized births (1.8% of births). A piecewise-constant input states the same
rate at every age in a band and, by the PATFR/D099 convention, at every parity
in a pooled band, so the user selected option (c) with 1-year age cells. The
input-band results above come from the first implementation and are kept in
Git history. [check-cell-resolution.R](../validation/milestone5/check-cell-resolution.R)
now confirms that wide, single-year and per-parity supplied bands give
identical results, and that supplying 0.25-year bands still yields 0.25-year
cells ([CSV](../validation/milestone5/cell-resolution-summary.csv)).

**Performance** ([benchmark](../validation/milestone5/benchmark-cell-spacing.R),
[CSV](../validation/milestone5/cell-spacing-benchmark.csv)): optimized
installed build (`-O2`), 50,000-person baseline aged up to 60, 100 years,
HFD-shaped single-year schedule for ages 12–54 plus an open 55+ band (one cell
under the tolerance above), median of three fresh-process runs. Absolute times
varied by about 3.5x between sessions on this machine with CPU frequency and
other load (midpoint 0.18 s in a quiet session, 0.65 s in the recorded CSV);
the cell/midpoint ratio was stable at about 3.4–3.7x. Builds from
`pkgload::load_all()` use `-O0` with library assertions and exaggerate the gap
(3.7 s versus 0.69 s), so time installed builds.

The remaining cost is per-woman membership updates: each woman crosses a cell
boundary every year of age while in a positive-rate band, about two million
updates in this case. Midpoint avoids them by integrating waiting times across
bands. An earlier version of this implementation took 7.2 s in the debug
build; binary-search band lookup, gaps for zero-rate bands, an allocation-free
clock heap and a lazy-deletion boundary heap reduced it without changing any
history.

**Review fixes (October 29, 2026).** A code review by the user's other agent
found that settlement used the new eligibility state for the preceding
interval, which dropped unrealized intensity after release and placed births
exactly at release (71 of 502 intervals in its one-woman experiment); that the
unreachable-cell check could loop forever on an upper-open band with infinite
earliest eligibility; and that cell births could precede a tied death. All
three are fixed with regression tests. The settlement fix changed cell-mode
histories only where a cell had no eligible member; the recovery tables above
were regenerated after it.

## Cells with no eligible women

If `Lambda_c > 0` but `W_c^e = 0` (no eligible member, or all eligible weights
zero), the cell cannot produce a birth. This happens briefly in initialized
cohorts and persistently when the input schedule implies more recent births
than the cell holds, which a B-year minimum interval makes impossible. The
cell keeps its intensity (decision 2, reporting revised by D128): when the
clock draws it, no birth occurs, nothing is carried forward, and one
unrealized birth is counted by one-year period from the run start, group,
union type, parity band and age band, so that users can see when inputs could
not be met. In a cell shared by groups, the group is drawn in proportion to its
members. Until November 4, 2026 the run recorded expected values
`integral Lambda_c dt` instead; realized births are unchanged in distribution.

## Interaction with existing features

- **Group multipliers** keep their meaning: they scale `m_c` before
  accounting, and make the schedules, and so the cells, group-specific.
- **Parity** cells are single actual parities. A pooled input band applies its
  rate to each contained parity, as in the PATFR and D099 conventions; for
  HFD's 5+ rate, actual parities 4, 5, 6, ... each target that rate.
- **Union types**: a woman who changes type during spacing carries her
  ineligible status into the new type's cell, so that cell's intensity covers
  her exposure. This resolves the D045 limitation by construction.
- **On-demand mode** keeps overall fertility; cells are one-year age sub-bands
  (and actual parities if schedules are parity-specific) per group.
- **Unstratified fertility** is the special case where cells are one-year age
  sub-bands pooled over parity.
  In a stationary population whose ineligible fraction equals the midpoint
  integral, the hazard equals D028's `f/(1 - b*f)` (example 1).
- **Matching calibration** uses each unpartnered eligible woman's hazard from
  rule 4 as her fertility-driven contribution. Because one firing or count
  change alters every member's hazard in a cell, calibration totals should be
  formed from cell intensities and the weight of unpartnered eligible members
  in each calibration bin, and must equal an explicit scan of rule 4.
- **Zero spacing** (`B = 0`): every member is always eligible, so
  `h_i = m_c * N_c * w_i / W_c`, which is `m_c` when all weights are 1.
- **Calendar-time rates (D118)**: a change that keeps the cell layout updates
  `m_c` in place and the clock continues; otherwise cells are rebuilt,
  preserving weights, parity and spacing (D128); see
  [calendar-varying rates](time-varying-rates.md).

## Individual weights (D102)

Weights are one by default. `hetfert = het_fert(cv, alpha)` enables the
[user-selected persistent lognormal weights](fertility-heterogeneity-notes.md). The user selected option A (decision 4):
weights set the probability of being chosen within a cell, not a direct rate
multiplier. Option B is retained here as the rejected alternative.

- **A. Target-preserving (selected).** `Lambda_c = m_c * N_c`. Weights
  decide *which* eligible women give birth, not how many births the cell
  produces, so observed cell rates still equal the inputs. Women with high
  weights spend more time ineligible; the accounting automatically raises the
  remaining eligible women's hazards to compensate. This fits inputs that are
  observed population rates, such as HFD parity-specific rates.
- **B. Weight-scaled (not selected).** `Lambda_c = m_c * W_c`. Weights act as rate
  multipliers in the SOCSIM sense, and observed rates equal `m_c` times the
  mean weight in the cell. Because high-weight women move to higher parities
  faster, mean weights drift by parity and age, so inputs are not recovered
  unless weights are calibrated separately.

## Worked examples

All rates are per woman-year; `B = .75` unless stated.

1. **Unstratified, stationary.** One age band with `m = .2` has 100 women, 15
   ineligible (`.75 * .2 = .15`). `Lambda = 20` births per year; each of the 85
   eligible women has hazard `20/85 = .2353`, the same as D028's
   `.2/(1 - .15)`. If a restart leaves only 5 women ineligible, the eligible
   hazard becomes `20/95 = .2105`, whereas D028 would keep .2353 and
   overshoot (the milestone 1 restart blip).
2. **Young high-parity cell.** Age band [18,19), parity 4+, `m = .4`, 10
   members of whom 8 gave birth within the last .75 years. `Lambda = 4`; each
   of the 2 eligible women has hazard 2.0. D099 instead uses
   `.4/(1 - .75*.4) = .571` for those 2 women, about 1.14 expected births per
   year, an observed rate of about .114 instead of .4.
3. **Birth moves a woman between cells.** A parity-1 woman in [25,26) gives
   birth at 25.4. The parity-1 cell loses one member; the parity-2 cell gains
   one ineligible member. Both intensities are recomputed. At 26 she moves to
   the [26,27) parity-2 cell, still ineligible, and becomes eligible at 26.15.
4. **Union change during spacing.** A partnered woman gives birth at 30.0 and
   is widowed at 30.3. She moves to the unpartnered cell as ineligible until
   30.75, so the unpartnered cell's intensity includes her exposure. Under D028
   her widowhood is handled by a separate precomputed adjustment that does not
   know she is ineligible.
5. **No eligible women.** Cell [17,18), parity 3+, `m = .4`, 3 members, all
   within .75 years of a birth. `Lambda = 1.2` cannot be realized; if this
   persists for half a year, on average .6 draws of this cell are counted as
   unrealized births. Handling per decision 2.
6. **Cancelled attempt.** In on-demand mode the parity-0 [22,23) cell fires
   and chooses a woman, but no eligible man is found and the birth is
   cancelled. Her parity and eligibility are unchanged, the fertility clock
   draws a new threshold, and the attempt is not replaced.
7. **Weights.** A cell with `m = .5` has 4 members: eligible women
   with weights 1, 1 and 2, and an ineligible woman with weight 3. Under
   option A, `Lambda = 2` and hazards are `2*w/4`: .5, .5 and 1.0. Under
   option B, `Lambda = .5*7 = 3.5` and hazards are `3.5*w/4`: .875, .875
   and 1.75.

## Validation plan

Validation follows from the rule; no scientific acceptance thresholds are set
here.

- **Accounting identities.** Maintained `N_c`, `W_c` and eligible totals equal
  explicit scans after every update type in the count-update table, at exact
  boundaries and after restarts. Calibration totals equal a scan of rule 4.
- **Analytical cases.** Constant-rate single cell without spacing (Poisson
  births at `m*N`); a stationary unstratified case against example 1; zero
  spacing equals the uncompensated schedule in distribution.
- **Recovery.** Over multiple replications, compare observed conditional rates
  with inputs and report means, MCSEs, exposure and unrealized births:
  initialized cohorts; a schedule change and restart (the milestone 1 Niger
  case); later windows of a generational population, including the age-[18,19)
  parity case; union-type transitions (the milestone 2 partnered case); group
  multipliers. The toy diagnostics in `validation/milestone5/` give the
  expected pattern.
- **Comparison with current behavior.** In stationary unstratified populations,
  results should agree statistically with D028; fixed-seed agreement is not
  expected.
- **Weights.** With weights fixed at 1, results agree statistically with the
  unweighted rule. Unequal test weights check rule 4's relative risks.
- **Independence and reproducibility.** Separate runs share no state; the
  same seed reproduces the same history.
- **Performance.** Benchmark against the current engine with the existing
  calibration benchmark workloads.

## Modeling decisions

These change the demographic model and required the user's decision.

All six were agreed by the user on October 29, 2026 and are recorded as D101;
decision 5 was revised by the user after validation.

1. **Population-dependent individual hazards.** A woman's hazard rises when
   others in her cell recently gave birth. This is a calibration device, not a
   behavioral model, and it is noisy in small populations or finely
   stratified cells. *Agreed by the user.*
2. **Cells with no eligible women.** (a) Drop the intensity and report
   unrealized births; (b) stop with an error; (c) carry the unrealized births
   forward until women become eligible, which changes their timing.
   *Agreed by the user: (a),* reported as counts since D128. This matches the existing treatment of partner
   scarcity; an error would make runs fail at random in small populations.
   See the causes below.
3. **Scope.** Add the new rule as an option alongside D028/D099 first, then
   make it the only rule once validated. *Agreed by the user.*
4. **Weight semantics.** *Agreed by the user: option A.* Weights determine the
   probability that an eligible woman is chosen within her cell; they are not a
   direct rate multiplier, so cell rates remain pinned to the inputs. The user
   initially considered rate-multiplier heterogeneity; see the comparison
   below. D102 subsequently selects lognormal weights through `het_fert(cv, alpha)`.
5. **Cell resolution.** *Revised by the user on October 29, 2026 after
   validation: option (c) with 1-year age cells.* Age bands are split into
   sub-cells of at most one year at whole ages and band edges, and pooled
   parity bands are split by actual parity, so the rate is recovered at every
   age and actual parity rather than only as a band average. The originally
   agreed input-band cells (option a) and 1-year cells with pooled parity
   (option b) were not selected. Narrower cells more often have no eligible
   woman, increasing unrealized births in small populations and at
   structurally infeasible young ages and high parities.
6. **Reporting.** Unrealized births and per-cell diagnostics as per-period,
   per-cell totals in run metadata, returned as a tibble. *Agreed by the user.*

### How a cell ends up with no eligible women

Only `N_c > 0` with no eligible member matters; an empty cell has no
intensity. Because parity k >= 1 can only be entered by a birth, every woman
arriving in a parity cell is ineligible for `B` years. A cell has no eligible
members whenever all of its current members arrived within the last `B` years.
This happens in three ways:

- *Start-up.* In a population initialized at parity zero, no woman can be at
  parity k and eligible before `k * B` years. Restarts from histories avoid
  this.
- *Sparse cells.* Young high-parity cells (for example [17,18) at parity 3+) or
  finely stratified cells may hold one to three women, often all recent
  arrivals. This is random and depends on population size.
- *Infeasible inputs.* If the input rates imply that arrivals during the last
  `B` years outnumber the cell's members, no population can meet the target.
  The composition analysis in the investigation note shows when this occurs.

**Observing unrealized births.** `calculate_fertility_rates(..., unrealized =
TRUE)` attributes these records to observation rows and adds an adjusted rate;
see [demographic analysis](demographic-analysis.md#unrealized-births-under-cell-accounting).
With the user's HFD 2000 parity inputs (50,000 people, years 2050–2150),
adding them back brought young high-parity cells to about the input rates, for
example parity 4+ at age 21 from .55 to .98 of input; they were about 0.1% of
all births.

**Advisory check for unreachable cells.** For parity-specific schedules with a
positive birth interval, `fertility()` (and so `set_fertility()`) warns when
positive rates fall in one-year age cells that no eligible woman can occupy,
under either spacing method. Births are singletons at least `B` apart, so the
earliest eligible age at parity k follows the schedule: the first positive
parity-0 rate gives the earliest first birth, eligibility at parity 1 starts
`B` later, the next birth is at the first positive parity-1 rate from that age,
and so on. With constant rates from 15 and `B = .75`, parity k is first eligible
at `15 + .75k` (parity 5 at 18.75). Union types are pooled, because women move
between them; groups are checked separately. Cells that are reachable for part
of their year are not reported, since every schedule has such a sliver at the
start of each parity. Open parity bands are checked at their first parity only:
higher actual parities in an open band always have unreachable young cells, so
those remaining losses appear only in the unrealized-births report. Positive
observed rates in such cells can be genuine (twins, intervals shorter than `B`,
births before the schedule's first age); the warning means the model cannot
generate them.

A related effect: when a cell has few eligible members, they receive large
hazards (example 2: hazard 2.0 at an input of .4). This is what recovers the
input rate, but it concentrates the cell's births among few women.

### Why option A rather than rate multipliers

Both options treat weights as multipliers among women in the same cell: an
eligible woman with weight 2 has twice the hazard of an eligible woman with
weight 1. They differ in what the input rate means.

- **Option B** treats the input as the rate for a woman with weight 1, and the
  cell level depends on its weights: `Lambda_c = m_c * W_c`. Observed rates
  equal `m_c` times the cell's mean weight. Women with higher weights reach
  higher parities sooner, so mean weights rise with parity and observed rates
  depart from the inputs, even if weights average 1 in the population.
- **Option A** treats the input as the observed average for the cell, as HFD
  rates are: `Lambda_c = m_c * N_c`. Weights redistribute births within the
  cell. It is option B with weights rescaled to average 1 within each cell.

With parity-specific inputs, much of what heterogeneity represents (women who
have had more children being more fertile) is already in the parity
conditioning of the data. Under option B it would be applied a second time;
under option A heterogeneity adds variation within cells while input rates are
still recovered. With unstratified inputs, option B changes ASFRs and TFR
unless weights are calibrated. Group multipliers already behave like option B
at the group level, but they make cells group-specific, so their targets
remain exact.

## Calendar changes and unrealized-birth accounting

Unrealized births are counted in one-year windows anchored at the
