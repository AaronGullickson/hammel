# United States example rates, around 2000

Published U.S. demographic rates from around 2000, prepared as input
tables for Hammel's process constructors. All rates are per person-year,
and ages are in years, with half-open bands `[age_start, age_end)`. Each
source is a U.S. federal statistical publication. Source files,
preparation scripts and further notes are in the package repository
under `validation/data/usa_2000_2001/`; `data-raw/usa_2000.R` builds the
datasets.

## Usage

``` r
usa_2000_mortality

usa_2000_fertility

usa_2000_fertility_union

usa_2000_marriage

usa_2000_migration
```

## Format

Tibbles with numeric `age_start`, `age_end` and `rate` columns, plus:

- `usa_2000_mortality`:

  44 rows; `sex` (`"f"` or `"m"`).

- `usa_2000_fertility`:

  6 rows; no other columns.

- `usa_2000_fertility_union`:

  12 rows; `union_type` (`"partnered"` or `"unpartnered"`).

- `usa_2000_marriage`:

  12 rows; no other columns.

- `usa_2000_migration`:

  132 rows; `origin` and `destination` regions, 12 directed pairs by 11
  age bands.

An object of class `tbl_df` (inherits from `tbl`, `data.frame`) with 6
rows and 3 columns.

An object of class `tbl_df` (inherits from `tbl`, `data.frame`) with 12
rows and 4 columns.

An object of class `tbl_df` (inherits from `tbl`, `data.frame`) with 12
rows and 3 columns.

An object of class `tbl_df` (inherits from `tbl`, `data.frame`) with 132
rows and 5 columns.

## Mortality

`usa_2000_mortality` gives death rates by sex for ages 0-1, 1-5,
five-year bands to 100, and an open 100+ band, for use with
[`mortality()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/mortality.md).
Rates are abridged from the single-year U.S. decennial life tables for
1999-2001 as life-table deaths divided by person-years lived in each
band (`l100 / T100` for 100+). Under Hammel's constant hazards within
bands,
[`calculate_life_table()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/calculate_life_table.md)
gives life expectancy at birth of 74.05 years for men and 79.38 for
women, against the source's 74.13 and 79.47.

## Fertility

`usa_2000_fertility` gives all-women birth rates for 2000 in five-year
bands from 15 to 45, for use with
[`fertility()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/fertility.md).
The published 10-14 and 45-49 rates are omitted, so the schedule sums to
2.049 births per woman rather than the published total fertility rate of
2.056.

`usa_2000_fertility_union` gives the same bands by `union_type`, mapping
legally married women to `"partnered"` and unmarried women to
`"unpartnered"`. Legal marital status does not distinguish cohabitation.
The 40-45 rates divide births to women 40 and older by women 40-44.

## Marriage

`usa_2000_marriage` gives female marriage rates in five-year bands from
15 to 70 and an open 70+ band, for rate-based initiation in
[`union_formation()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/union_formation.md).
They are the published annual proportions of unmarried women who married
in the year before a 2001 survey, used directly as search rates per
person-year without conversion to hazards. Marriages include first
marriages and remarriages. Search attempts can fail to find a partner,
so simulated marriage rates need not match these.

## Migration

`usa_2000_migration` gives approximate migration rates between the four
census regions (`"NE"` Northeast, `"MW"` Midwest, `"S"` South, `"W"`
West) by age, March 1999 to March 2000, for
[`group_transition()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/group_transition.md)
on a region dimension with these categories. Each origin-destination
rate is the all-ages flow divided by the origin's Census 2000
population, scaled by the national age pattern of interstate moves. The
same age pattern is applied to every pair, which understates older-age
moves to the South. The rates apply to individuals; see
[`household()`](https://aarongullickson.github.io/hammel/reference/household.md)
for moving family members together.
